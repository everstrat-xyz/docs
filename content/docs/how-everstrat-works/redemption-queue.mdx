This page explains the full lifecycle of a queued redemption. For the user-facing summary, see
[Exiting & redeeming](/using-everstrat/exiting-and-redeeming).

## When does an exit go to the queue?

When you call `exit()`, the AMM first checks its **free balance**: the ETH it holds minus any ETH
already reserved for pending claims (`freeBalance = address(AMM).balance − lockedForClaims`). If the
free balance is enough to pay your redemption at the current base price, the exit settles
immediately. Otherwise your request enters **ExitQueue** and your EVE is escrowed on AMM.

At the [dated snapshot](/operations/current-deployments#exit-liquidity-at-this-block),
free balance was **zero**: all AMM ETH was reserved for existing claims, and the keeper's
immediate-exit float top-up was disabled. Deposits go to Controller and do not add to AMM's
free balance, so new exits queue.

There is a minimum size for queued exits: your redemption must be worth at least **0.001 ETH** (the
default `minBatchExitETH`). The admin can raise this up to **0.05 ETH** if needed to keep batch sizes
economical. Immediate exits have no minimum.

## Step-by-step: queued exit lifecycle

<Steps>
  <Step>
    **Your request joins the current batch.** Your EVE tokens are escrowed in the AMM (not yet
    burned). ExitQueue stores the request metadata and the base price at submission for the
    slippage check later.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    **A keeper prices the batch.** The keeper calls the Controller, which prices the batch on the
    ExitQueue using `AMM.eveBasePriceInETH()`. Pricing moves that batch's outstanding ETH liability
    and escrowed EVE out of active NAV and live supply. Once priced, the batch is locked; requests inside it
    cannot be cancelled until the batch processing window expires.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    **The keeper processes each request.** For each request in the batch, the ExitQueue checks whether
    the settled price has fallen too far from the price at submission time. If `finalEvePrice` has
    dropped more than your `priceTolerance` below `evePriceAtRequestTime`, the request is **closed for
    slippage**. Your EVE is returned to you and you receive no ETH. Otherwise, your ETH entitlement is
    computed and credited.

    Processing must occur within three days of batch pricing. Strictly after that deadline,
    an unprocessed request expires and cannot be settled; cancel it to recover EVE.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    **Your ETH is credited to your claimable balance.** The ETH is not pushed to your wallet
    automatically. Instead, it is added to `claimableBalances[you]` and tracked in the AMM's
    `lockedForClaims` counter (which is why it is excluded from the free balance and from NAV). This
    **pull-over-push** pattern ensures one user's reverting wallet cannot block the entire batch from
    settling.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    **You call `claim()` to withdraw.** Once your ETH is in your claimable balance, you call `claim()`
    on the AMM at any time to transfer it to your wallet.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Slippage tolerance

When you queue an exit, you specify a `priceTolerance`: the maximum percentage drop in base price you
are willing to accept between your submission and the batch settlement. If the price falls more than
this, your EVE is returned and you can try again.

The tolerance must be in the range `[0, 100%]`. Setting it to 0 means any price drop will trigger a
slippage return; setting it high means you accept significant price movement in exchange for a higher
chance of your exit settling.

## The escape hatch

If your batch is priced but the keeper never processes your request (for example, because the protocol
encounters a problem), cancellation becomes available **strictly more than 3 days after pricing**.
You can call `cancelRedemption(batchId)` on the AMM to recover your EVE. This is the escape hatch
(`MAX_BATCH_PROCESSING_TIME = 3 days`).

After that window, processing reverts with `ExitQueueBatchExpired`. Waiting longer cannot
make the original unprocessed request payable. Cancellation returns EVE; any later redemption
requires a new exit request and a new quote. At exactly three days, processing is still allowed
and cancellation is still blocked.

You can also cancel **at any time before the batch is priced**. Unpriced batches have no
waiting period.

| When | Can you cancel? |
| --- | --- |
| Before the batch is priced | Yes, at any time |
| After batch is priced, up to and including 3 days | No, the request is still inside the processing window |
| More than 3 days after pricing, still unprocessed | Yes, through the `cancelRedemption` escape hatch |
| Request already processed | No. Claim the credited ETH instead |

<Callout type="info">
  The 3-day window after pricing exists to give the keeper time to process all requests in the batch
  without users gaming the system by watching the final price and cancelling if they don't like it.
  The escape hatch applies to each still-unprocessed request. Once a request is processed,
  its ETH remains claimable even if the user has not claimed it within three days.
</Callout>

## Summary of key parameters

| Parameter | Default | Maximum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minimum queued exit size | **0.001 ETH** | **0.05 ETH** (admin-configurable) |
| Escape hatch after pricing | **Strictly more than 3 days** | Fixed in this implementation |
| Maximum live priced batches | **25** | Fixed in this implementation |

At the [dated deployment snapshot](/operations/current-deployments), the Mimic queue keeper required
a batch to be **24 hours old before automatic pricing**, counted from batch creation.
A request joining an older batch does not start a fresh 24-hour clock. This is
an executor scheduling parameter, separate from ExitQueue's three-day window after pricing.
Keeper availability, gas limits, and liquidity affect actual timing. See
[Keepers and automation](/developers/contracts/keepers-and-automation).
